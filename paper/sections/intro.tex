Video anomaly detection (VAD) systems are increasingly deployed at the
edge — on cameras, robots, and mobile devices — where compute, memory,
and power are all constrained and predictions must be produced online, as
frames arrive, rather than after the fact. This motivates two properties
that are jointly under-studied in recent work: \emph{strict causality}
(a prediction at time $t$ must depend only on frames $1,\dots,t$, with
bounded per-step compute and memory) and \emph{real hardware
feasibility} (measured, not asserted, latency/throughput on the class of
device the method targets).

State-space models (SSMs), and Mamba~\cite{gu2023mamba} in particular,
have recently been proposed for VAD on efficiency grounds: VADMamba
\cite{vadmamba2025} combines a Mamba-based temporal block with
vector-quantized frame prediction and optical-flow reconstruction, and
Wave-MambaAD~\cite{wavemambaad2025} adds a wavelet-driven SSM for
unified subtle/large-scale anomaly detection. These works establish that
SSMs are a good fit for VAD's efficiency requirements, but (a) still
process the input in clips or windows rather than truly online per-frame,
(b) report no theoretical relationship between the model's temporal
memory and how quickly it can detect an anomaly, and (c) benchmark
efficiency in terms of GPU throughput rather than measuring the
target-deployment hardware directly.

This paper makes three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A strictly causal streaming formulation of SSM-based VAD, with a
  concrete architecture (Section~\ref{sec:method}) in which every
  operation is expressible as a per-frame $O(1)$ update.
  \item A theoretical analysis (Section~\ref{sec:theory}) relating the
  recurrence's decay spectrum to detection delay and minimum-detectable
  anomaly duration, validated empirically.
  \item An end-to-end evaluation including real on-device latency,
  memory, and throughput measurements on consumer edge silicon
  (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}), in addition to standard accuracy
  metrics on three benchmarks.
\end{enumerate}
